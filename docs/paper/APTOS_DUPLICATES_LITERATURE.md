It says the train and public-test label distributions differ. https://speakerdeck.com/hoxomaxwell/aptos-2019-28th-place-solution
- **Kaggle thread: some test images also exist in train. [search-summary]** Replacing predictions for those leaked images with train.csv labels moved the leaderboard score only from 0.821 to 0.820, so the overlap was too small to matter for the score. Page text could not be fetched; the exact count is unverified. https://www.kaggle.com/c/aptos2019-blindness-detection/discussion/104754

- **A competition solution repo deduplicated the 2019 train set. [verified, no count]** README: "duplicate images removed from the 2019 train subset", with a cleaned label file `trainLabels19_unique.csv` and a script `2019_train_find_dublicates.py`. - **Kaggle write-up "4th place solution (2nd after LB cleaning)". [search-summary]** A commenter asked whether the winner fixed duplicate images in the 2019 train set; no clear answer was seen. https://www.kaggle.com/c/aptos2019-blindness-detection/discussion/107926

- **Dataset-audit claim [search-summary only, page fetch timed out twice]:** a paper from RUDN University repository reportedly compared file hashes across more than 100 Kaggle fundus/DR sources and found 56% of images repeated at least twice across datasets, with resized copies in 9 datasets that hashing could not catch. ## 2. How papers handle it, and with/without-dedup comparisons

- **I found no APTOS paper that reports results both with and without deduplication, and no QWK or accuracy delta. [unverified / not found]**
- Practitioners removed duplicates from train (repo above). The only quantitative hint is the Kaggle leakage thread (0.821 to 0.820), which is a leaderboard effect of fixing leaked test predictions, not a dedup ablation. [search-summary]
- A 2026 arXiv paper that merges APTOS-2019 with other DR datasets says its deduplication procedure is in Appendix B.6; I could not read the appendix, so how it handles duplicates and counts are unverified. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13015
- A 2022 uncertainty-aware DR paper uses APTOS only as an out-of-distribution test set, and splits its training data so images from one patient stay in one split; it does not mention duplicate removal. [search-summary] https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.09042
- Analogous cases outside DR where dedup and re-evaluation were done: ImageNet duplicates with differing labels (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.04596, search-summary), CIFAR near-duplicate purge "ciFAIR" (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.00423, title only; I recall small accuracy drops but did not verify numbers).

## 3. General literature on duplicate leakage in medical imaging

- **Dermatology audit, Scientific Data 2025: DermaMNIST/HAM10000 and Fitzpatrick17k. [verified]** HAM10000: 10,015 images from 7,470 lesions; 13.47% of lesions (1,006) span more than one partition; 18 further duplicate pairs found by fastdup and manual review, 7 of them leaking across partitions. Fitzpatrick17k: 2,297 duplicate clusters, some with conflicting labels; the verified test set shrank from 348 to 215 after cleaning, and accuracy fell from 26.7% to 4.65% (the authors caution the partitions are not directly comparable). They released corrected splits and code. https://arxiv.org/html/2401.14497v2
- **OCT leakage, Scientific Data 2022. [verified]** Per-image splits versus per-volume/subject splits inflated accuracy by about 5 to 30 points (MCC +0.07 to +0.43; Srinivasan accuracy 0.899 vs 0.603; AIIMS 0.978 vs 0.935). Kermany v2 had about 92% of test images from subjects also in train. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9500039
- **Leakage across ML-based science, Patterns 2023. [search-summary]** Leakage found in at least 294 papers across 17 fields; proposes model info sheets. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10499856
- **Secondary-data audit of Kaggle DR studies (IJRASET). [verified]** 30 study rows using APTOS/EyePACS; explicit leakage-control reporting in 12 of 30 (the table shows 12 yes, 17 partial, 1 no, while the abstract text says "partially described in 9", so the paper is internally inconsistent); patient/image split described in 2 of 30 (3 partial, 25 no/unknown); external validation 9 of 30; confidence intervals 2 of 30. The paper names "duplicate handling" as a leakage source. https://www.ijraset.com/best-journal/validation-and-reporting-reliability-in-publicdataset-diabetic-retinopathy-ai-studies
- **Review of DR deep learning (arXiv 2511.11065). [search-summary]** Says APTOS 2019 appears in at least 15 surveyed works and that per-image rather than per-patient splits inflate metrics; EyePACS results use differing splits and cleaning. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.11065
- **What reviewers and journals expect.** CLAIM 2024 asks authors to state how data were assigned to training, tuning and testing partitions and prefers the term "internal testing" for a held-out split of the training source. [search-summary] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11304031 . I could not confirm any CLAIM or TRIPOD+AI item that names duplicate images explicitly; the exact item wording is unverified.

## 4. Do typical ~80-85% random-split APTOS papers mention duplicates?

- Evidence is indirect. The IJRASET audit (30 rows) found split/leakage description sparse, with patient/image-split logic clearly described in only 2 of 30. Other APTOS split papers surfaced in search (for example a 90/10 split) describe the split without mentioning deduplication. [search-summary] I did not read a systematic sample of 80-85% papers, so "most do not mention duplicates" is a reasonable inference but not a measured fraction. [unverified]
- Note that APTOS has no public patient IDs, so patient-level grouping is impossible; image-level exact/near-duplicate removal is the only available leakage control.

## Implication for us

Practice in the field is overwhelmingly to ignore duplicates, but the reviewer-facing direction (dataset audits, CLAIM-style reporting, the DermaMNIST/Fitzpatrick corrections) is to disclose and correct them. The only APTOS-specific evidence found is that duplicates exist and that some practitioners dedup train, and that fixing leaked test images changed the Kaggle score by about 0.001 (search-summary). Our 19 of 367 overlap (5.2%, with 3 conflicting grades) is small, so the headline numbers should barely move, which makes disclosure cheap. Reporting the 361-image clean result alongside the 367 reference, with the exact counts and the method (SHA-256 exact match, which does not catch re-encoded or resized copies), is better aligned with where good practice is heading than keeping 367 silent, and the paper can say that prior APTOS work rarely reports this. Frame the 367 result as the standard-protocol number comparable with prior work and the 361 as the leakage-controlled number; report the delta, and state that near-duplicates were not searched (a perceptual-hash pass would strengthen the claim).
